What is and is not claimed about these logs
-------------------------------------------

These are PUBLIC SAMPLE LOGS, not PRODUCTION TRAFFIC. The distinction is
deliberate and is maintained throughout this repository.

The upstream repository describes both files only as sample files accompanying a
getting-started example. It makes no claim that either is captured production
traffic, and it documents nothing about how the traffic was collected, whether
any consent was obtained, or whether it was anonymised. This project therefore
claims nothing beyond "public sample log" about their origin. The provenance of
the underlying real-world traffic is UNKNOWN.

Two verified properties bound how these logs may be used, and both are corpus
limitations rather than incidental details:

  * Degenerate timestamps. The minute field is always "05" in apache_sample_1.log
    and is "05" or "06" in nginx_real.log. Every sessionised session therefore
    spans at most 59 seconds and all inter-arrival times are artefacts of the
    upstream generator. No claim about realistic temporal behaviour may rest on
    these files.

  * nginx_real.log is demo data. Its entire URL universe is three placeholder
    paths (/downloads/product_1, product_2, product_3) and 65.8% of its responses
    are 404. Despite its file name it is not a real package mirror's logs.

Personal data
-------------

Neither log carries authentication data, cookies, session identifiers,
credentials or tokens; the authuser field is empty in all 61,462 records.

Both carry CLIENT IP ADDRESSES, which are real, public and unmasked (1,753
distinct in W1; 2,660 in W2). These are personal data in most jurisdictions. The
logs are NOT anonymised and must not be described as such. See DATASET.md
section 8 for the handling obligations that follow.

W1 additionally contains 198 email-shaped strings, all of them crawler-operator
contact addresses embedded in bot User-Agent strings (7 distinct addresses, the
standard "Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; <bot>; <contact>)" convention). These are
published contact points for automated agents, not end-user personal data.
